What I've built

ControlLoop-RT: MPC, feedforward and real-time safety

One DC servo plant taken from first principles to a real-time, fault-tolerant control loop. View the repo

  • PID diagnosed as unstable, replaced by pole-placed PD: 6.06% overshoot, 0.447 s settling. Feedforward cut tracking error by 87.4%.
  • Constrained MPC (do-mpc/CasADi, 20-step horizon at 50 Hz): 14× less chatter than PD under noise, and a documented limit: its velocity constraint breaks under an unmodeled disturbance.
  • Ported to a 1 kHz Zephyr RTOS task in C, with a watchdog (fault detected in about 4 ms), dual-sensor voting (200 of 200 faulty samples caught, 0 false alarms) and an active-hold safe state.
  • 600-trial benchmark and a documented, not certified, ISO 26262 hazard analysis.

TrackLoop: vehicle path tracking

In progress

Four path-tracking controllers on a car model, following paths from my own Hybrid A*–RRT* and Reeds-Shepp planners, benchmarked the same way as ControlLoop-RT.

  1. Vehicle modelsKinematic and dynamic bicycle models of a car
  2. Four controllersPure Pursuit, Stanley, LQR and linear MPC
  3. Paths from my plannersHybrid A*–RRT* and Reeds-Shepp paths as the references to follow
  4. BenchmarkLateral and heading error, steering effort and smoothness, at three speeds, with and without model errors
  5. Stability analysisBode plots and gain and phase margins, and where each controller goes unstable as speed rises
  6. System identificationEstimate the vehicle model from noisy data, then re-test the controllers on it
